Different Solar Collectors

Explore different types of solar collectors, their working principles, applications, and key characteristics.

Flat Plate Collector

A flat plate collector uses an absorber plate to capture solar radiation and transfer the heat to a circulating fluid. It is commonly used for domestic hot water and low-temperature heating.

Evacuated Tube Collector

An evacuated tube collector uses vacuum insulation around the absorber to reduce heat loss. It provides high thermal performance and is suitable for hot-water and heating applications.

Parabolic Trough

A parabolic trough uses curved reflective mirrors to concentrate sunlight onto a receiver tube. It is commonly used for high-temperature solar thermal applications.

Linear Fresnel Collector

A Linear Fresnel collector uses multiple mirror strips to reflect and concentrate sunlight onto a fixed receiver. It can be used for industrial heat and steam generation.

Compound Parabolic Collector

A compound parabolic collector uses reflective surfaces to collect sunlight over a range of incoming angles and concentrate it toward an absorber.

Heat Pipe Collector

A heat pipe collector transfers thermal energy through sealed heat pipes using evaporation and condensation. It is useful for efficient solar heat transfer.

Thermosyphon System

A thermosyphon system uses natural circulation caused by temperature and density differences. It is commonly used for domestic solar water heating.

Integrated Storage Collector

An integrated storage collector combines solar heat collection and thermal storage in one system. This makes it useful for compact solar water-heating systems.

Unglazed Collector

An unglazed collector does not use a glass cover and is generally designed for low-temperature applications such as swimming-pool heating.
